Our team at Common DevOps is the independent 3rd party that published the full investigation of the Node-IPC hack in May 2026. 

For anyone unfamiliar with Node IPC, it's a software component that helps other components communicate. 
Technically, it's a nodejs module for local and remote Inter Process Communication (IPC), Neural Networking, and able to facilitate machine learning.
It has over 2 billion lifetime downloads and continues to accumulate millions more per month.

Here is how the attack worked:
One person's domain name expired. An attacker bought the domain name and recreated the email address that had access to release official updates. They then published malicious updates that stole passwords.

Think of the trust you place in routine software updates. I automate my updates and fully expect an improvement. Having an update that steals passwords, is a scenario that extends further than Node-IPC. That is what makes this more than a problem for one software project. Do we reconsider trusting every update? What would happen in your organization if an important trusted employee had their identity stolen?

In open-source communities, it's even trickier. We often rely on strangers. And, projects can be stable for years between updates. This hacking pattern is likely to occur more frequently, as AI automates finding these gaps.

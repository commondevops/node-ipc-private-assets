Our team at Common DevOps is the independent 3rd party publishing the post-incident report for the Node-IPC hack in May.

For anyone unfamiliar with Node-IPC, it's a nodejs module for local and remote Inter Process Communication (IPC). It's useful for Neural Networking, and able to facilitate machine learning.  

Simplistic explanation: 
It's a software component that helps other components communicate with each other. Widely popular, this is a high-value target. It has over 2 billion lifetime downloads and continues to accumulate millions more per month.

Here is how the attack worked:
One person's domain name expired. An attacker bought the domain name and recreated the email address that had access to release official updates. They then published malicious updates that stole passwords.

Think of the trust you place in routine software updates. I automate my updates and fully expect an improvement. Having an update that steals passwords, is a scary situation and a scenario that extends far beyond this specific hack. That's what makes this more than a short-term problem. Do we reconsider trusting every update? What happens in your organization, if an important trusted employee had their identity stolen?

In open-source communities, it's even trickier. We often rely on strangers. And, projects can be stable for years between updates. This hacking pattern is likely to occur more frequently, as malicious people using AI get better at finding & exploiting these gaps.
